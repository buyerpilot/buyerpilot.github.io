Start with product fit, not supplier badges

Remove listings that do not match your material, specification, design or expected order quantity. A supplier comparison is only useful when the underlying products are genuinely comparable.

Compare the price that applies to your quantity

Alibaba and 1688 listings can show multiple quantity tiers. BuyerPilot uses the intended order quantity so the shortlist is not based only on the lowest headline price.

Use supplier signals together

Review available seller tenure, ratings, repeat-purchase information, business type, marketplace status, Trade Assurance or merchant indicators together. No single badge should be treated as a guarantee.

Check manufacturer and trading-company indicators

Business type can help frame your questions about production capability, customization and supply-chain structure, but it should be verified during supplier communication and due diligence.

Move from shortlist to real verification

After shortlisting, confirm quotations, MOQ, sample terms, lead time, certifications, product compliance, factory claims and payment protections before placing an order.

Frequently asked questions

Common questions

Does BuyerPilot verify that a China supplier is legitimate?

No. BuyerPilot organises and ranks available Alibaba and 1688 marketplace information to help with shortlisting. Buyers should still verify supplier claims, samples, contracts and commercial details.

Can BuyerPilot replace a factory audit or third-party inspection?

No. Marketplace research and physical or documentary verification solve different problems. BuyerPilot helps with the research stage before deeper due diligence.

What supplier signals should I compare before ordering samples?

Common signals include product fit, quantity-aware price, supplier tenure, ratings, repeat-purchase information, business type, marketplace status, relevant certifications and the quality of supplier communication.
